How to Date Safely at University

Dating at university should be exciting, enriching, and enjoyable—never dangerous or stressful. Whether meeting matches from apps or asking out someone from class, here are 12 essential, practical campus safety rules every student should follow.

The Unique Safety Realities of University Life

For most students, entering university represents their first taste of total personal independence. Away from parental oversight, you manage your own schedule, social life, and romantic decisions. However, this newfound freedom also brings distinct vulnerabilities: living in off-campus hostels, unfamiliar cities, navigating alcohol at campus parties, and meeting people from online dating apps.

Dating safely does not mean being fearful; it means being intentional, prepared, and confident in your boundaries. By embedding practical safety protocols into your dating life, you protect your physical, emotional, and financial wellbeing while enjoying everything campus romance has to offer.

Rules 1–4: Pre-Date Screening and Digital Privacy

1. Verify Profiles Before Meeting: Before agreeing to meet anyone in person, confirm their authenticity. On platforms like The College Date, look for verified profile photos screened by automated Vision AI. If you met on generic social media, suggest a quick 2-minute video call in-app or ask for mutual campus friends to ensure they are who they claim to be.

2. Keep Initial Chats In-App: Avoid rushing to share your personal WhatsApp number, Instagram handle, or home address. Messaging inside the app allows you to get to know someone while keeping your personal contact information secure. If they turn out to be disrespectful, you can block them in-app without changing your phone number.

3. Never Share Sensitive Financial or Account Data: Romance scammers target students with urgent stories about tuition emergencies, medical bills, or transport problems. Never send money, Mobile Money transfers, airtime, or cryptocurrency to someone you have not met and known well. Never share bank details or account recovery security answers.

4. Protect Your Physical Privacy: Never post or share your exact hostel room number, daily running routes, or photos showing identifying details outside your residence building.

Rules 5–8: First Meeting Protocols — Location, Daylight, and Transparency

5. Choose Bustling, Daylight Public Spaces: Your first two or three meetings must always occur in busy public areas with high foot traffic. Great choices include campus cafeterias, student centre plazas, library coffee shops, or popular shopping centre cafes during daytime hours.

6. Never Agree to an Isolated First Date: Never meet a new date in a private hostel room, secluded dorm room, isolated vehicle, or private off-campus apartment. If someone insists on meeting in a private space instead of a public cafe, cancel the date immediately.

7. Inform a Trusted Friend or Roommate: Always share your date's name, profile photo, meeting location, and an agreed check-in time with a trusted hostel roommate or close friend. Send a quick text when you arrive and when you are safely heading back.

8. Control Your Own Transportation: Never rely on your date to pick you up or drop you off. Walk with friends, take the campus shuttle, or use trusted ride-hailing services (Uber, Bolt, Yango) with active trip-sharing enabled. Knowing you have your own way home ensures you can leave whenever you wish.

Rules 9–12: Consent, Food/Drink Safety, and Gut Instinct

9. Keep an Eye on Your Food and Drink: Never leave your beverage or meal unattended, even for a quick trip to the restroom. If you leave the table, order a fresh drink upon your return. Never accept open cups or unsealed beverages from strangers.

10. Understand Enthusiastic, Continuous Consent: Consent must always be voluntary, mutual, clear, and ongoing. You have the absolute right to set physical boundaries, say "no" at any moment, or change your mind at any stage of a date. Anyone who pressures, guilts, or ignores your boundaries does not respect you.

11. Watch Out for Love-Bombing and Manipulation: Be cautious if someone showers you with excessive compliments, professes deep love within days, demands constant texting updates, or tries to isolate you from your campus friends. Healthy romance develops gradually with mutual respect.

12. Trust Your Intuition and Have an Exit Strategy: If your gut tells you something is wrong, do not rationalize the feeling. You do not need a polite excuse to leave. Say: "I need to head out now—thank you for your time," and walk directly to a campus security post or populated public space. Save your campus security hotline numbers on your phone's speed dial.

Campus Emergency Resources and Reporting

Every university maintains dedicated safety resources for students:

  • Campus Security Division: Familiarize yourself with emergency security call boxes, 24-hour campus patrol numbers, and safe-walk shuttle services across campus grounds.
  • Student Health Services: Access confidential healthcare, reproductive health services, and medical consultations on campus.
  • Student Counselling Centres: If you experience relationship trauma, harassment, or emotional distress, campus psychologists and trained counsellors provide free, confidential guidance.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the safest place for a first date on a university campus?

The safest location is a well-lit, bustling student centre, campus cafeteria, or busy library plaza during daytime hours where security personnel and fellow students are present.

What should I do if my date refuses to meet in public?

Do not go. Anyone who refuses a public first meeting and insists on an isolated or private venue is ignoring basic safety boundaries and should be blocked.

Is it okay to leave a date early if I feel uncomfortable?

Yes, 100%. Your safety and wellbeing always come first. You do not owe anyone your time, and you can leave immediately without feeling guilty.

How do I report someone who harassed me on The College Date?

Tap the three dots on their profile or in your chat conversation, select "Report User," and specify the issue. Our moderation team reviews reports and bans abusive accounts.
