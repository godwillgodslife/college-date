How to Spot a Catfish in University

Online dating and campus social apps are great for meeting new people, but fake profiles, stolen pictures, and malicious catfishes still lurk across unmoderated networks. Here is how to spot a catfish in university, protect your identity and money, and verify who you are really talking to.

The Reality of Campus Catfishing

Catfishing—creating a deceptive online profile using someone else's photos and a fabricated identity—is not just an issue for celebrities. Across university campuses, catfishing happens frequently for several reasons:

  • Financial Cybercrime: Scammers targeting students with fabricated romance stories to solicit "urgent 2k," M-Pesa, MoMo transfers, or transport fare.
  • Insecurity & Social Anxiety: Students who feel self-conscious about their appearance and steal influencer photos to experience online validation.
  • Campus Malice or Spying: Ex-partners or acquaintances creating fake accounts to spy on classmates, monitor romantic interests, or gather screenshots.

7 Warning Signs of a Campus Catfish

Watch out for these telltale indicators of a fake dating profile:

  1. Influencer / Model Photos with Zero Campus Context: Their profile features high-fashion studio modeling photos, but not a single casual picture on campus, in a lecture hall, or with student friends.
  2. Persistent Excuses to Avoid In-Person Meetups: They always have an elaborate excuse why they cannot meet for a 20-minute coffee on campus—illness, travel, sudden hostel lockdowns, or family emergencies.
  3. Refusing a Quick Video Call: When you suggest a brief 2-minute video call in-app, their camera is suddenly "broken," their connection is "too poor," or they claim to be camera shy.
  4. Rushing Conversations Off-Platform: They try to move you immediately away from the app to unmoderated messaging channels before you have established trust.
  5. Vague Details About Their University Life: They cannot name specific lecturers, campus buildings, or departmental landmarks that any genuine student at that institution would know.
  6. Sudden Financial Solicitations: Any request for emergency funds, exam registration fees, airtime, or transport fare is an immediate red flag.
  7. Excessive Love-Bombing: Professing intense emotional attachment, declaring you their "soulmate," or making grand romantic promises within days of matching.

Actionable Steps to Verify a Match's Identity

Before investing your heart or agreeing to meet, verify authenticity with these simple tactics:

  • The 2-Minute Video Call Rule: Before meeting in person, request a brief 2-minute video call. If someone refuses to show their face live on video, do not meet them.
  • Ask About Common Campus Landmarks: Ask casual, localized questions: "Which lecture theatre do you usually have your economics classes in?" or "Do you prefer the coffee at the student union or the library cafeteria?" A scammer from another city will trip over basic campus details.
  • Search Their Social Footprint: Look for consistent, active campus social profiles with mutual student friends.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I do if I suspect a profile is a catfish?

Cease communication immediately, do not send money or sensitive photos, take screenshots for evidence, and report the profile using the in-app reporting button.

Why do catfishes refuse video calls?

Because a live video call instantly shatters the illusion by revealing that the real person behind the camera does not match the photos on the profile.

Should I confront a catfish before blocking them?

No. Confrontation rarely yields a truthful confession and can lead to verbal abuse. Simply report and block the account quietly.
