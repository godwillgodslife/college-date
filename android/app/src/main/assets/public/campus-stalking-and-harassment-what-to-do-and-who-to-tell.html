Campus Stalking and Harassment: What to Do, How to Document, and Who to Tell

Defining Campus Stalking and Harassment Under Institutional Law

Quick-Take Standard: Campus stalking is a repeated pattern of unwanted surveillance, non-consensual contact, or intimidating behavior that causes a student reasonable fear for their personal safety or emotional well-being. It spans cyberstalking, physical loitering at hostel gates, following between lecture halls, and persistent messaging after explicit rejection.

On university and polytechnic campuses, romantic persistence is sometimes dangerously romanticized in student folklore as determination or courting. However, there is a clear, legal distinction between mutual flirtation and obsessive harassment. The moment an individual communicates a clear, unambiguous refusal, any continued unwanted contact constitutes harassment.

Under the Violence Against Persons Prohibition (VAPP) Act and higher education disciplinary statutes, stalking is recognized as a punishable offense that warrants immediate suspension, rustication, and criminal prosecution. Victims often suffer severe academic disruption, anxiety, and sleeplessness while trying to appease or quietly avoid their pursuer.

Recognizing the pattern early and refusing to normalize invasive behavior is the most critical step toward regaining absolute peace and security on campus. Stalkers rely on social passivity and shame to maintain control over their targets, assuming students will hesitate to make a public fuss.

By shifting your mindset from self-blame to proactive defense, you reclaim agency. Stalking is never caused by being too friendly, dressing attractively, or being polite in initial lecture conversations. The fault lies entirely with the perpetrator who refuses to accept boundaries.

Common Forms of Campus Stalking: Physical and Digital Invasions

Campus stalkers frequently exploit the communal layout of tertiary institutions where class schedules, hostel allocations, and faculty movements are relatively predictable.

Common manifestations include:

  • Hostel and Lodge Loitering: Waiting for hours outside your hall of residence, lodge gate, or department entrance under the guise of casual coincidence.
  • Digital Surveillance and Cyberstalking: Creating burner social media accounts to monitor your WhatsApp statuses, Instagram stories, and comments after being blocked.
  • Lecture Hall Shadowing: Showing up at lectures or tutorial centers completely unrelated to their course of study simply to maintain physical visual proximity.
  • Third-Party Infiltration: Approaching your coursemates, roommates, or departmental course representatives to extract your personal phone number, home address, or timetable.
  • Unsolicited and Intimidating Deliveries: Sending unwanted gifts, food parcels, or cryptic notes to your room through delivery riders despite explicit requests to stop.
  • Reputational Smears and Rumor-Mongering: Spreading malicious falsehoods among mutual friends or campus forums to isolate you socially and make you feel dependent on their approval.

Strategic Action Matrix: Counterproductive Reactions vs Authoritative Protection

Initial Unwanted Outreach Politely laughing it off or offering false excuses like being too busy. Delivering one clear, written, unambiguous statement of refusal and boundary.
Continued Digital Contact Engaging in back-and-forth arguments or debating the rejection. Ceasing all replies, capturing timestamped screenshots, and archiving evidence.

Evidence Preservation Deleting disturbing voicemails and texts out of panic or distress. Backing up chat logs, call records, and security footage to encrypted cloud drives.

Implementing an authoritative protocol deprives the stalker of the emotional reaction they seek while creating an indisputable paper trail for university authorities and legal counsel.

How to Build an Indisputable Incident Documentation Log

When presenting a case of harassment to campus security, university disciplinary panels, or law enforcement, subjective impressions carry less weight than a chronological, documented evidence dossier.

Maintain an incident log recording:

  1. The Chronological Incident Diary: Record the date, exact time, location, and specific actions of the perpetrator for every single encounter, along with names of nearby witnesses who observed the interaction.
  2. Digital Artifacts: Save unedited full-screen screenshots displaying phone numbers, social media handles, dates, and timestamps. Never crop screenshots, as uncropped images maintain forensic integrity.
  3. Audio and CCTV Records: If confrontations occur in commercial campus areas or hostel lobbies with security surveillance, request that hostel porters or store owners preserve footage from that specific time window.
  4. The Cease-and-Desist Notice: Retain a copy of your single, explicit message stating: I am formally stating that your attention is unwanted. Do not call, text, follow, or approach me. Further contact will be reported to university security and police.
  5. Protecting Your Digital Privacy: Review your WhatsApp privacy settings to hide your Last Seen, Status, and Profile Picture from non-contacts. Avoid tagging real-time campus locations on Instagram or TikTok while attending events.

Official Escalation Pathways: Who to Notify on Campus

You do not have to fight stalkers alone. University structures have specialized departments mandated to protect student welfare and enforce campus discipline.

Contact these official resources:

  • Hall Porters and Wardens: If staying in an on-campus hall, inform the Chief Porter. Porters maintain guest logbooks and have the authority to bar unauthorized visitors from entering the premises or loitering near female halls.
  • Campus Security Division: File a formal written complaint with the Chief Security Officer (CSO) of the institution. Campus security can issue an official warning and summon the perpetrator for a formal undertaking of peace.
  • Dean of Student Affairs (DSA): The DSA oversees student disciplinary boards. Stalking constitutes gross misconduct that routinely leads to disciplinary suspension or expulsion under tertiary codes of conduct.
  • University Gender and Harassment Units: Most tertiary institutions host dedicated Gender-Based Violence (GBV) desks or sexual harassment committees offering confidential support and counseling.
  • External Law Enforcement and Legal Aid: Under the VAPP Act and Cybercrimes Prohibition Act, campus stalking can be prosecuted through the state Police Command or National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) with criminal penalties.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if unwanted attention has crossed the legal line into criminal stalking?

Unwanted attention crosses the line into stalking when someone persists in following, messaging, watching, or contacting you after you have explicitly told them to stop, causing you fear, anxiety, or emotional distress.

What should I do if an ex-partner refuses to leave my hostel gate or off-campus lodge?

Do not go outside alone to confront them. Alert hostel porters, your lodge caretaker, or security immediately. Call trusted friends to stay with you, document their presence with photo evidence, and file an official security report.

Will reporting a student stalker to campus security or Dean of Student Affairs affect my privacy?

Campus security and Student Affairs units handle stalking investigations under strict confidentiality protocols to safeguard victims from retaliation, social stigma, or academic compromise.

How can I adjust my daily campus routine to minimize exposure while an investigation is pending?

Walk to lectures in groups of coursemates, alter your walking routes across campus, study in well-monitored faculty libraries rather than secluded corners, and keep your live location shared with a close roommate.
