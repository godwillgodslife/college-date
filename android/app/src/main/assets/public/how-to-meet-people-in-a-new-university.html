How to Meet People in a New University

Arriving at a new university—especially in a city where you don't know a single soul—can feel overwhelming. Here is a battle-tested guide for freshers, international students, and transfer undergraduates on how to meet people, build a solid friend group, and feel at home fast.

The First 48 Hours: Settling In and Overcoming Initial Shock

Stepping foot onto a new campus where you know nobody is an emotionally intense milestone. Whether you just moved across the country, arrived from a small town, or transferred from another college, the first 48 hours can trigger homesickness and social sensory overload.

The first step is normalizing the feeling: almost every new student around you is pretending to feel more confident than they actually are. Don't sit in your room spiraling. Unpack your essentials, take a 45-minute walk around campus to locate the main library, student centre, and nearest grocery shop, and take deep breaths. Settling in is a gradual process.

Hostel and Residence Life: Turning Strangers into Allies

Communal accommodation is your greatest social asset during the first two weeks:

  • The Open-Door Habit: When you are inside your room unpacking, playing music, or browsing during the day, prop your door open. It signals approachable warmth and encourages corridor neighbours to say hello.
  • Communal Kitchens & Dining Halls: Don't eat all your meals locked in your bedroom. Sit at communal dining tables and ask: "Hey, mind if I join you? What faculty are you in?"
  • Organize a Group Grocery Run: Ask your floor mates: "I'm heading to the local market / supermarket to grab laundry soap and groceries—anyone want to walk together?" Shared errands break the ice effortlessly.

Navigating Academic Spaces: Departmental Groups and Tutorials

Academic connection forms the backbone of student friendships:

  • Join Official Departmental Group Chats: Make sure you are added to your course's official WhatsApp or Telegram groups. These channels are invaluable for tracking lecture timetables and assignment clarifications.
  • The Sit-Next-to-Someone Rule: For the first month, challenge yourself to sit next to someone different in every lecture and introduce yourself before the professor starts talking.
  • Form Study Pods Early: After the first tutorial, suggest forming a 3-person study group to review weekly problem sets. Academic collaboration naturally evolves into weekend coffee and lunch hangouts.

Campus Societies, Sports, and Campus "Third Spaces"

University life outside the classroom is where lifelong passions and connections ignite:

  • Attend Societies Sign-Up Day: Sign up for two clubs: one related to your major (e.g., Computer Science Club, Debate Union) and one purely for personal enjoyment (e.g., Photography, Hiking, Dance, or Gaming).
  • Campus Faith Fellowships or Cultural Groups: Many students find instant community and mentorship through campus religious fellowships or cultural associations representing their home region.
  • Find Your Campus Third Space: Find a comfortable spot outside your hostel and lecture halls—a specific library nook, student cafe, or courtyard lawn—and spend downtime there regularly so your face becomes familiar to regular visitors.

Pacing Yourself: Quality Over Speed

Remember that genuine friendships take time to develop. Sociological research suggests it takes roughly 50 hours of shared time to move from acquaintance to casual friend, and over 200 hours to form a close friendship. Do not panic if you don't have a "best friend" by week three.

Stay consistent, be kind, keep showing up to campus activities, and your circle will form naturally.

Frequently Asked Questions

What if I am a transfer student and everyone already has friend groups?

Focus on joining campus societies, student committees, and departmental projects where new collaborative groups form every semester regardless of academic year.

How do I deal with feeling homesick during my first month?

Stay connected with family and old friends via phone calls, but push yourself to spend daylight hours in campus social and study areas rather than isolating in your room.

Can I use The College Date to connect with other freshers?

Yes! The platform lets you select your academic level (such as 100L / 1st Year) and set your intention to "Find Friends" so you can match with fellow freshers.

What should I do if my roommates and I do not get along?

Maintain polite, respectful boundaries in the room, and invest your social energy in making friends through lectures, campus clubs, and student societies outside your hostel.
